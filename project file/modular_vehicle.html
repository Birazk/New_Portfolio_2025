Modular Electric Vehicle Chassis for Rural Nepal

A low-cost, locally manufacturable vehicle platform with four interchangeable configurations, designed for a complete role change in under 60 minutes. Funded by the Royal Norwegian Embassy through the Energize Nepal Program.

The challenge

Car makers are moving from fixed platforms to modular systems that offer more variants faster and cheaper. But the regions that need flexible transport most, rural areas with limited infrastructure and fast-growing populations, are the ones the global auto industry serves least.

The goal was a modular electric vehicle whose chassis is light enough to keep inertia low, stiff enough for precise handling, and strong enough to survive fatigue loads from passengers, the powertrain, and rough roads, all while being buildable with local tooling.

Design method
  • Measurement: took precise measurements of an existing chassis and components with calipers and tape as the basis for the CAD model.
  • 3D modeling: built the chassis, battery pack, and motor in SolidWorks 2023 R1 using extrusions, cuts, fillets, and chamfers.
  • Assembly: assembled the chassis, suspension, battery pack, motor, wheels, and axles to check fit and interference.
  • Simulation: evaluated structural integrity in ANSYS and produced detailed drawings with dimensions, tolerances, materials, and finishes.
  • Boundary conditions: four supports at the suspension mounting points, two at the front and two at the rear axle.
Crash analysis · explicit dynamics

A 50 mph frontal impact was simulated with a uniformly distributed 2000 kg impactor mass to check crashworthiness: the front structure should crumple and absorb energy while preserving a survival space for occupants. The maximum deflection was 46.411 mm.

Harmonic and modal analysis

Modal analysis finds the chassis's natural frequencies and mode shapes so the design avoids resonance on bumpy roads; harmonic analysis checks its response to repeated vibration.

The highest harmonic amplitude occurred near 50 Hz (0.14752 mm, 9187.1 N), indicating resonance at that frequency. Across the modes the deflection ranged from 4.64 mm to 7.57 mm, confirming a rigid frame.

Static structural analysis

With a constant, uniformly distributed 2000 kg load, the maximum deflection was only 0.55036 mm and the peak equivalent stress 41.41 MPa, well below the material's yield strength.

Results
  • Stiff and strong: elastic strain stayed at 0.00033812 mm/mm and maximum shear stress at 22.8 MPa, so the material remains elastic.
  • Durable: fatigue analysis predicts a virtually infinite life (10⁶ cycles).
  • Safe margin: the safety factor ranges from 2.09 to 15, well above the minimum of 1.
  • Buildable: the space-frame layout avoids expensive tooling, enabling decentralized local manufacture and cheaper repairs.
