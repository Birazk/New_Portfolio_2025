Sustainable Manufacturing Practices in the Hydropower Industry

The challenge

Hydropower is renewable, but manufacturing turbines, penstocks, and dams is energy-intensive and carbon-heavy. The review gathered modern practices that could make the whole manufacturing life cycle sustainable.

My role · co-author
  • Searched and synthesized literature on sustainable manufacturing and materials.
  • Helped build the review's framework of trends, gaps, and best practices.
  • Wrote sections on advanced manufacturing and regulatory standards.
  • Drafted, reviewed, and refined the manuscript with the team.
Key research areas
  • Life cycle assessment: a cradle-to-grave view, from raw materials to decommissioning, pinpoints the highest-impact stages.
  • Advanced processes: additive manufacturing cuts material waste on complex turbine parts; optimized CNC toolpaths and low-power states cut energy use.
  • Sustainable materials: recycled steel for penstocks, eco-efficient concrete with recycled aggregate, and bio-based hydraulic lubricants.
Impact
  • A cohesive framework for sustainable hydropower manufacturing.
  • Highlights the role of standards such as ISO 14001.
  • A foundation for future research, published in a peer-reviewed series.
