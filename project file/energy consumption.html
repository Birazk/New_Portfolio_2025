On-Road EV Energy Consumption and Range

A field study of the real-world energy use and range of a converted EV on hilly roads, published in IOP Conference Series: Earth and Environmental Science (2024).

Objective

Range anxiety is fueled by the gap between lab-rated and real-world range, especially in hilly regions like Nepal. This study measured that gap on the road and built a more accurate predictive model.

My role · co-author and data analyst
  • Experiment design: equipped the converted EV with GPS and BMS logging.
  • Data processing: ran on-road tests over a route with large elevation changes and synchronized the data in MATLAB, Python, and Excel.
  • Modeling: built a physics model of aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance, and kinetic and potential energy.
  • Insight: identified key correlations and produced the paper's visualizations.
Key findings
  • Terrain dominates: altitude and state of charge show a strong negative correlation (−0.89), so hill climbing is a leading cause of battery drain.
  • Regeneration matters: route energy loss fell from 20.86 kWh to 10.22 kWh with regenerative braking.
  • Accurate prediction: the model's final-SOC prediction was within 6% of the measured value.
